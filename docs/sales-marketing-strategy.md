# Sales & Marketing Strategy

## Purpose

Operating playbook for how sales and marketing run day-to-day for the Ad Generation Engine. Sits underneath the GTM strategy (which defines *where we play*) and above the outreach scripts (which define *what we say*). Read this if you are deciding pipeline stages, content priorities, conversion benchmarks, or retention motion.

Update this doc each time the funnel math, ICP, or offer materially changes.

## Strategic Frame

The Ad Generation Engine is a **general-purpose** product — campaign brief in, ads out, for any vertical. Sales and marketing, however, operate **vertically**: we sell into one well-researched buyer category at a time, because that is how a small operator beats horizontal incumbents (Canva, AdCreative.ai, agencies). HVAC SMBs in DFW are the cycle-1 vertical. The motion below is engine-agnostic and re-runnable; the *messaging, prospect list, and sample assets* are the parts that get rebuilt per vertical.

Within the cycle-1 vertical, the bottleneck is **trust**, not awareness. HVAC owners get pitched daily by marketing agencies and view most of them as overpriced and detached from the trade. The same will be true of roofing, plumbing, and any other trade vertical we open next.

Our sales and marketing motion has to do three things at once, in any vertical:
1. Earn enough trust on a single 90-second call to book a meeting.
2. Show competence in a sample, before any contract or commitment.
3. Stay close enough after the close that the customer renews on month two without a fight.

Everything below is built around those three outcomes. Anything that does not contribute to one of them is noise.

## Sales Strategy

### Pipeline Stages

| Stage | Definition | Exit criteria | Target dwell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Researched** | Prospect added to the playbook with brand, audience, pain points, channels filled in | Outbound touch attempted | <7 days |
| **1 — Engaged** | Prospect picked up, replied to email, or requested info | Discovery call booked OR explicit no | <14 days |
| **2 — Discovery booked** | 15-min call on the calendar | Call held | <10 days |
| **3 — Sample reviewed** | Prospect has seen branded sample images on the call | Verbal interest + pricing presented | Same call |
| **4 — Proposal sent** | Stripe link or signed agreement out | Card charged OR explicit no | <5 days |
| **5 — Closed–Won** | Card charged, kickoff scheduled | First asset delivered | <7 days |
| **5b — Closed–Lost** | Hard no, no-show twice, or 30 days silent | Move to nurture or kill | n/a |

**Stalled = anything past the target dwell.** Every Monday, every stalled deal either advances or moves to Closed–Lost. No deal sits in stage 1 for 6 weeks.

### Qualification — the 4 questions on every discovery call

Forget BANT. For HVAC SMBs, only four signals matter:

1. **Are they spending on ads now?** ($1.5K–$15K/mo across LSA + Meta = fit)
2. **Who decides on marketing?** (Owner = good. Office manager = need to escalate. "I'll have to ask my partner" = stall risk)
3. **Are they on a long agency retainer?** (Locked-in $3K+/mo = disqualify)
4. **What does their current creative look like?** (Stock photos / phone shots = perfect; agency-produced templates = harder)

If 3 of 4 are yes/positive, present the $497 Campaign tier and ask for the close on that call. If only 1–2 are positive, send the sample and follow up — do not pitch tier yet.

### Conversion Benchmarks (cycle 1 — calibrate after 30 days)

| Step | Target rate | What "off" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Researched → Engaged | 60% | Below 40% = playbook research is shallow |
| Engaged → Discovery booked | 40% | Below 25% = opener is wrong |
| Discovery → Proposal | 60% | Below 40% = sample isn't landing or pricing is wrong |
| Proposal → Closed-Won | 50% | Below 30% = we're chasing wrong-fit shops |
| **Researched → Won (overall)** | **~7–10%** | 20 prospects → 2 paid logos |

These are HVAC-SMB-specific benchmarks, not B2B SaaS averages. They will look low to anyone used to PLG funnels. They are correct for cold outbound to local trades.

### Close Motion — Take the Card on the Call

Cold-outbound SMB sales dies in the gap between "yes" and "let me think about it." The default close is:

1. Walk through the sample (5 min)
2. Present Campaign tier as the recommendation, not as one of three options (3 min)
3. Send the Stripe link *while still on the call* — "I'll send the link now, charge goes through today, first batch of images ships Friday"
4. Stay on the line until they confirm payment

If they need to "talk to their partner," set a hard 48-hour follow-up before hanging up: *"What time Wednesday should I call back to lock this in?"* Anything looser than that is a soft no.

### Sales Enablement — What Must Exist

Build these in this order. Skip anything not on this list.

| Asset | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Per-prospect sample images (3 per shop) | Get on the call, demonstrate competence | In flight via the engine |
| One-page sample deck (PDF) | Sent post-call, reinforces sample | TODO |
| Stripe payment links per tier | Close on the call without sending an invoice | TODO |
| Two-paragraph service agreement | Lightweight, not a 10-page MSA | TODO |
| Onboarding email (3 emails over 7 days) | Set expectations, prevent month-1 churn | TODO |
| Customer reference list (live as soon as logo #2 closes) | Social proof on later calls | After logo #2 |
| Loom screencast of the engine producing a campaign (90 sec) | Used in cold email when phone fails | After logo #3 |

### CRM / Tracking

Do not buy HubSpot. Use a Google Sheet with these columns until you have 30+ active deals:

`Prospect | Stage | Last touch | Next touch | Owner DM (Y/N) | Currently spending ($) | Notes | Source`

Review weekly. Sheet grows with the business; HubSpot or Pipedrive at logo #15 if it actually saves time.

## Marketing Strategy

### Positioning — Two Layers

Positioning is built in two layers: an engine-level statement that stays constant across every vertical, and a vertical-level statement that gets rewritten when we open a new buyer category.

**Engine-level (constant):**
> The Ad Generation Engine turns a structured campaign brief into a complete, channel-ready ad bundle — strategy, copy variants, image prompts, and rendered images — in minutes instead of hours. Vertical-agnostic by design, sold through verticalized go-to-market motions.

**Cycle-1 vertical (HVAC):**
> For local HVAC contractors spending $2K–$15K/month on ads with stale creative, the Ad Generation Engine delivers a monthly campaign-in-a-box — branded ad images, copy, and landing snippets — at a fraction of an agency retainer. Unlike DIY tools that require the operator to design ads themselves, and unlike agencies that bundle creative inside a $3K+/mo retainer, we deliver done-for-you creative at a productized price.

The vertical-level statement is the source of truth for every piece of HVAC marketing copy. When vertical #2 opens, write a new vertical-level statement using the same template (buyer → spend band → pain → outcome → unlike-DIY-unlike-agency contrast).

### Messaging Pillars (HVAC — Cycle 1)

Each vertical gets its own three messaging pillars derived from the prospect playbook for that vertical. Within a vertical, every email, post, ad, and call reinforces one of the three. Anything off-pillar gets cut.

**HVAC cycle-1 pillars:**
1. **"Your reputation deserves better creative."** — speaks to legacy/family/long-tenured shops (Jade Air, Harlen Johnson, Cowboy Cooling, Dial One Johnson)
2. **"Hyper-local ads built for *your* ZIP codes, not generic Dallas."** — speaks to neighborhood-focused shops (Prime AC, Unlimited, Apple A/C, EMG, Cozy Home)
3. **"Turn your reviews into ads that convert."** — speaks to high-rated shops (5 Star, Red Star, Berry Best, Advanced Texas Air)

Map every prospect to one pillar in the playbook so every touch is consistent. When a new vertical opens, derive its three pillars by clustering the prospect list along the same axes (legacy / hyper-local / proof-of-quality almost always recur, but the language changes — e.g., roofing's "quality" pillar leans on workmanship warranties, not Yelp stars).

### Marketing Channels — What We Run, What We Don't

#### Run now
- **Branded sample-image generation per prospect.** This is our highest-leverage marketing asset; it doubles as sales tool. Scale this before anything else.
- **LinkedIn proof-of-work posts (1–2 per week).** Post the actual generated samples for shops we've worked with (with permission), tagged with the metro and the angle. Audience is partly future customers, partly potential operator-partners.
- **Referral asks at month 2 of every paid customer.** Specifically: "Who else in [adjacent ZIP] could use the same?" — never "anyone you know."

#### Run after logo #5
- **One short case study per quarter.** 400 words, one chart, one quote. Posted on a simple landing page and referenced in cold emails.
- **HVAC trade Facebook group seeding.** Texas-only operator groups. Share generated samples as "look what we made for [shop]," not as ads.
- **One vertical podcast appearance per quarter.** Trade podcasts (e.g., "HVAC Sellutions," "Service Business Mastery"). Audience overlaps perfectly.

#### Do not run
- Paid ads on Google or Meta (no CAC model, no payback period to defend the spend)
- SEO content marketing (12-month payback, wrong tool for a seasonal cold-outbound motion)
- Trade show booths (high cost, low conversion for solo founder)
- Cold LinkedIn outbound (HVAC owners do not live on LinkedIn — phone wins)

### Lead Magnets — Two, Not Ten

Resist the urge to build a content library. Two lead magnets cover the entire funnel for cycle 1:

1. **"Sample summer campaign for your shop"** — the per-prospect generated sample. This is the magnet *and* the demo *and* the proof of work. Highest ROI artifact in the entire business.
2. **"DFW HVAC Ad Creative Audit" (one-page PDF)** — used after the call as a leave-behind. Walks through what's wrong with their current creative and what we'd do differently. Generates urgency without being pushy.

### Brand and Visual Identity

Two layers, mirroring positioning.

**Engine-level (ProPlan Solutions house brand):**
- Sender name on every email: "Theo Torku — ProPlan Solutions"
- No logo-heavy email signatures. Two lines of text, max.
- No marketing automation footers ("Unsubscribe," "View in browser") in cold email — it screams cold blast.

**Vertical-level (HVAC sample-image style guide for cycle 1):**
- Photorealistic, real-trade aesthetic
- Avoid uncanny AI human faces, brand color overlays only
- Texas/local visual cues (homes, technicians in branded shirts, suburban backdrops)
- The vertical brand should read as: *competent local Dallas operator who happens to use AI* — not: *AI startup that happens to serve HVAC*

When opening a new vertical, write a new vertical-level visual style guide. The engine's image provider settings (size, quality, format) stay the same; the *aesthetic direction* in the brief is what changes.

## Funnel Model

Math for the first 90 days inside the cycle-1 HVAC vertical. The conversion rates below are vertical-specific (HVAC SMB cold outbound). When opening a new vertical, expect the *shape* to repeat but recalibrate the rates against the first 20 worked prospects.

### Cycle 1 (Days 0–30)

| Stage | Volume | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Researched prospects (existing playbook) | 20 | — |
| Engaged | 12 | 60% |
| Discovery booked | 5 | 40% |
| Proposal sent | 3 | 60% |
| Closed–Won | 1–2 | 50% |
| **MRR added** | **$500–$1,000** | — |

If cycle 1 closes 0 logos from 20 well-worked prospects, the offer is wrong — not the market. Adjust the offer (price, deliverable mix, tier structure) before adding more prospects.

### Cycle 2 (Days 31–60)

| Stage | Volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New researched prospects | 20 | Adjacent ZIPs / referrals |
| Re-engaged from cycle 1 | 5 | Stage 1–2 stalls revisited |
| New paid logos | 2–3 | Compounding from referrals |
| Cumulative MRR | $1,500–$3,000 | First renewal happens this cycle |

### Cycle 3 (Days 61–90)

| Stage | Volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New researched prospects | 30 | Roofing vertical opens at 5+ HVAC logos |
| Paid logos cumulative | 5–7 | First case study published |
| Cumulative MRR | $3,000–$5,000 | First inbound lead from LinkedIn proof-of-work expected here |

### Sanity Checks

If at Day 30 we have:
- **0 logos** → offer problem. Stop dialing, fix the offer, then resume.
- **1 logo, 1 hr/week to service** → great. Push outbound harder.
- **2+ logos but per-customer time >3 hr/week** → productization problem. Pause new sales. Fix delivery before logo #4.

## Retention and Expansion

Every paid logo is rare and expensive to acquire. Treat retention as a top-of-funnel investment, not a customer success afterthought.

### Month 1 — Anchor the Relationship
- Day 1: kickoff call, confirm brand assets and angle
- Day 7: first batch delivered, walk through on a 15-min call
- Day 21: mid-month check-in — "what's resonated, what hasn't"
- Day 28: invoice/renewal posture — quietly confirm card on file works

The goal of month 1 is not perfect creative. It is the customer feeling like *something happens every week*. Frequency of contact > polish of any single deliverable.

### Month 2 — The Renewal Conversation
At day 35–40, schedule a 20-min review:
- What's gotten clicks / calls (if measurable)
- What angles to try next
- One ask: a referral to a non-competing shop in an adjacent ZIP

Customers who give a referral in month 2 churn at half the rate of those who don't. Always make the ask.

### Month 3+ — Expansion Levers

| Lever | When to introduce |
|---|---|
| Add-on campaign ($150 one-time) | Month 2, around a seasonal event (back-to-school, fall maintenance) |
| Tier upgrade (Campaign → Authority) | After two months of Campaign tier with engaged customer |
| Annual prepay (2 months free) | Month 3 renewal moment |
| Multi-location pricing | When customer mentions a second franchise or location |

### Churn Signals — Watch For

- Skipped or canceled monthly check-in call
- No reply to a delivery email within 5 days
- Customer asking "are you sure this is working?" — not a churn signal yet, but the start of one. Get a measurement story in front of them within 7 days.

## Quarterly Priorities

### Q1 (Days 0–90) — Validate and Repeat
1. Close 5–7 paid HVAC logos from the playbook
2. Lock the $497 Campaign tier as the anchor
3. Build the 7-asset sales enablement kit
4. Publish 1 case study, 12+ LinkedIn proof-of-work posts

### Q2 (Days 91–180) — Productize and Expand Vertical
1. Open roofing as vertical #2 by re-running this playbook against the same engine — new prospect list, new sample assets, new messaging pillars; no engine changes
2. Ship the asset delivery pipeline (Drive + auto-email) — benefits all verticals
3. First annual prepay deals (target: 30% of new closes)
4. Launch the "DFW Creative Audit" lead magnet (HVAC); template a parallel audit asset for roofing

### Q3 (Days 181–270) — Scale the Motion
1. Hire/contract a part-time SDR or VA for outbound at logo #15
2. Inbound from LinkedIn + case studies = 25% of pipeline
3. Self-serve brief intake (web form → engine) live for new customers
4. First decision point: bootstrap path vs. partnership conversation with vertical SaaS

## Decision Triggers

Pre-commit to these so the path forks happen on data, not vibes:

- **Kill the offer and rebuild** if 30 worked prospects produce 0 closes
- **Raise prices to $697** if the first 5 closes happen with zero pricing pushback
- **Add a setup fee** if month-1 churn exceeds 1 in 5 logos
- **Stop outbound and productize** if Theo's per-customer service time exceeds 3 hr/week at logo #8
- **Open vertical #2** when HVAC reaches 5 paid logos with <1 churn

## Open Questions

Revisit monthly:

- Is the $497 anchor the right price, or are we leaving money on the table at $697?
- Does adding the landing-page snippet actually drive renewal, or just inflate scope?
- Is LinkedIn proof-of-work generating any inbound, or is it pure brand?
- When does Theo's calendar break — at logo 8, 12, or 20? (This determines the SDR hire date.)
- Should we build a thin client portal for delivery, or stay on Drive folders forever?
